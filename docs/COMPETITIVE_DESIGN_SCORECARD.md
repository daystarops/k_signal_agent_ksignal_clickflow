# Competitive Design Scorecard

## Overall score

**7.2 / 10**

The notes are a useful Western-media brief, not yet a complete competitive design system. They correctly protect K-Signal from imitation and clutter, but they under-specify discovery, engagement metadata, mobile behavior, and the Korean-native environments that supply much of K-Signal's source context.

## 1. Summary

**Score:** 7.5 / 10  
**Strengths:** Clear synthesis: hierarchy, legibility, headline energy, and a calmer bilingual identity.  
**Weaknesses:** Treats three Western publishers as the whole comparison set and does not define measurable outcomes.  
**Approve / Revise / Reject:** **Revise.** Keep the positioning; add Korean-native behavior and discovery goals.

## 2. NYT Lessons

**Score:** 8.3 / 10  
**Strengths:** Strong on hierarchy, trust, typography roles, spacing, issue context, and editorial grouping. Fresh inspection confirmed strong desktop/mobile prioritization, read-time metadata, useful search filters, and a mature site index.  
**Weaknesses:** Does not mention search result structure, byline/date/read-time consistency, corrections, or the cost of NYT-scale density.  
**Approve / Revise / Reject:** **Approve with revisions.**

## 3. BuzzFeed Lessons

**Score:** 7.8 / 10  
**Strengths:** Correctly identifies format legibility, payoff-led headlines, and card noise. Fresh inspection confirmed explicit trending rank, reactions, comment counts, strong format/category cues, and a noisy commerce/content mix.  
**Weaknesses:** Misses the importance of visible engagement numbers and ranking as navigation; says what to avoid more clearly than what to adapt.  
**Approve / Revise / Reject:** **Revise.** Borrow legible engagement and format cues selectively.

## 4. Vice Lessons

**Score:** 7.6 / 10  
**Strengths:** Accurate on concrete nouns, tension, confident verbs, and attribution. Fresh inspection confirmed compact categories, prominent author/date/tags, and aggressive related-content recirculation.  
**Weaknesses:** Understates subscription friction and the risk that large recirculation modules bury the article ending. No visible comments were found in the sampled article.  
**Approve / Revise / Reject:** **Revise.**

## 5. Footer / Publication Architecture

**Score:** 8.4 / 10  
**Strengths:** Sensible, honest, right-sized publication links; correctly rejects fake account/business infrastructure.  
**Weaknesses:** Needs Editorial Standards, Corrections, source policy, and archive links on the roadmap. The current generated footer already implements About, Contact, Privacy, Cookie Settings, Accessibility, Terms, and copyright.  
**Approve / Revise / Reject:** **Approve with a staged roadmap.**

## 6. K-Signal Recommendations

**Score:** 7.4 / 10  
**Strengths:** Protects the existing lead/secondary/supporting hierarchy, bilingual lanes, reading width, receipts, Pagefind, and mobile calm.  
**Weaknesses:** “Restrained related-story packaging” is too vague. It does not separate archive-wide relevance from issue navigation, define metadata priorities, or specify category/topic/entity destinations.  
**Approve / Revise / Reject:** **Revise.** Adopt the two-system article discovery architecture.

## 7. Do Not Do

**Score:** 8.7 / 10  
**Strengths:** Clear guardrails against copied visual language, fake infrastructure, community chaos, and ungrounded claims about “what Koreans think.”  
**Weaknesses:** Add: do not expose popularity without provenance; do not treat comment volume as truth; do not let translation replace original labels/receipts; do not make same-issue proximity equal relevance.  
**Approve / Revise / Reject:** **Approve with additions.**

## 8. Missing Korean-native platform perspective

**Score:** 2.8 / 10  
**Strengths:** The notes preserve the Korean/English bridge and warn against flattening Korean opinion.  
**Weaknesses:** FMKorea, Naver, and TheQoo are absent. That omission leaves out board taxonomies, HOT/ranking paths, recommendation/view/comment signals, anonymous reply culture, portal-to-news movement, mobile list compression, login walls, and auto-translation failure modes. For a publication interpreting Korean internet culture, this is a structural gap.  
**Approve / Revise / Reject:** **Reject as complete coverage.** Retain the Western analysis but pair it with `KOREAN_PLATFORM_UX_NOTES.md`.
